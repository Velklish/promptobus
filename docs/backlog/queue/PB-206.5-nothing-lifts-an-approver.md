# PB-206.5 · The fourth role is addressable, rated and reserved, and nothing lifts it

- **Order:** 75
- **Scope:** `lib/spawn.js` (`RESERVED_WORKER_PREFIXES`), `lib/liftoff.js`, `lib/cli.js` (the command list and its help), `docs/reference/03-cli.md`, `docs/reference/16-…` of the consumer is **not** this card's business
- **Created:** 2026-09-13, found by a consumer-side worker writing the acceptance procedure
- **Dependencies:** none

## Context

[PB-206](../../archive/PB-206-approver-is-not-a-participant/task.md) made acceptance a fourth addressed role and carried it end to end: the protocol grammar, the store, the MCP surface, the role lists of model routing and their schemas, the quality floor, the routing policy. Its ADR says the task orchestrator lifts an approver for one piece after a green review.

**No command does that, and the obvious substitute is closed on purpose.**

- `lib/liftoff.js:13` carries the role in the lift naming map, so the machinery knows the word.
- `lib/spawn.js:401` lists `approver` in `RESERVED_WORKER_PREFIXES` — `spawn --worker approver` is refused deliberately, which is right: an approver is not a worker.
- `lib/cli.js` mentions the role once, in the `--role` flag of `models`. No command creates an address of it.
- The released pin a consumer actually runs still answers `unknown recipient address … — orchestrator, worker:<slug> or reviewer:<slug>`; that half is expected and resolves with the next release.

So the state is: a role that can be addressed, rated, routed and denied, and that cannot be brought into existence. Every text describing the acceptance procedure has to be written in a tense that says "this is the order" rather than "this is what happens", and the consumer's own card had to do exactly that.

> Source: 2026-09-13, a worker writing the consumer's acceptance procedure. Verified independently: `grep` over `lib/cli.js`, `lib/spawn.js`, `lib/liftoff.js` and the installed package's protocol.

## Work to do

- Decide who lifts an approver and by what command. Two shapes are visible and the card does not prejudge: a verb of its own beside `spawn` and `review`, which matches how the reviewer is lifted today; or a flag on the existing lift, which [ADR-012](../../adr/adr-012-stopping-one-participant-is-a-verb-of-its-own.md) refused for a different subject and whose reasons should be re-read before reusing them.
- State what the lift requires as a precondition, if anything. The consumer's procedure says an approver is not lifted before the piece's review is green; whether the mechanism can hold that, or whether it stays the caller's discipline, is a decision of this card — the package forms no verdicts ([ADR-009](../../adr/adr-009-reviewer-resolves-no-discrepancy.md)), and a precondition it cannot check must not be written as if it could.
- Say where the approver works. A reviewer gets a read-only directory; a worker gets a worktree. An approver merges, runs gates on the merged tree and archives, so its directory is neither.

## Out of scope

- The consumer's procedure texts: written already, and deliberately in the descriptive tense for exactly this reason.
- The role's rights, floor and routing: decided by PB-206 and not reopened here.

## Checks

- A live run in which an approver address is brought into existence by a command and sends a message that reaches the orchestrator and a worker.
- `spawn --worker approver` still refuses, with the same words, after the new path exists.
- The consumer's texts can drop the descriptive tense: a grep finds no sentence there promising an order the mechanism cannot perform.
