# PB-234.3 · Nothing notices a probe recorded against a tree the hand-off has already left

- **Order:** triage
- **Area:** `schemas/v1/handover-record.schema.json`, the send-time coherence check in `lib/handoff.js`
- **Created:** 2026-09-17, finding from the PB-234 run
- **Parent:** [PB-234](../queue/PB-234-probe-record-must-name-target-verdict.md)

## Measurement

The handover record carries two shas: `tree` — the tree being handed over — and `checks.mutationProbe.tree` — the tree the probe actually ran against. Nothing compares them. They can differ silently, and during the PB-234 run they did: the evidence artifact described a probe measured on one sha while every other section named a later one. A human reviewer caught it by reading two attached files against each other.

That is the expensive way to catch it. Both values sit in the same document, in named fields, and the comparison needs nothing from outside — exactly the shape the parent card gave `expected` against `reddened`.

## Why it matters more than a stale label

HEAD moves during a review round: a fix lands, a finding card is filed, a branch is rebased. Each move leaves the probe behind unless it is re-run. A record whose `mutationProbe.tree` lags its `tree` is claiming a probe of code that is no longer the code being accepted — and the claim reads as clean, because every field is present and well-formed.

The failure is not that the probe was wrong. Often the code under mutation is untouched by the moves, and the probe is still meaningful. The failure is that the record cannot say which of the two it is, so a reader must reconstruct it from prose, or trust.

## What to do

Compare the two in the same door that already checks the record's coherence, beside the schema pass:

- `checks.mutationProbe.tree` equal to `tree` — the record is self-consistent, nothing to say.
- They differ and the record does not explain it — refuse, naming both shas.
- They differ and the record says why — accept. A probe deliberately kept on an earlier sha is legitimate when the moves since then did not touch the mutated code; the record should be able to state that rather than being forced to re-run.

The third branch is what keeps this from becoming a rule that costs a full probe on every docs-only commit. It needs a field to carry the explanation; naming it is part of the work.

## Not in scope

- Proving the probe ran at all. The record is written after the run by its author; this check compares two of its own claims, nothing more.
- The release-skew seam — a new field cannot be sent until the version carrying it ships. That is [PB-234.2](PB-234.2-new-record-field-unsendable-until-released.md), and any new field here inherits it.

## Checks

- A record whose probe sha differs from its tree sha, with no explanation, is refused at `send`, with both shas named.
- The same record carrying the explanation is accepted unchanged.
- A record whose two shas agree is unaffected.
- A record written before this field exists keeps passing, or its migration is named explicitly.
