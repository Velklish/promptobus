# PB-229.1 · A short quiet period before the first knock would fold a hand-off burst into one postcard

- **Scope:** `src/supervisor.ts` (the first knock of a fresh mailbox, `KNOCK_COALESCE_SEC`), [03-cli](../../reference/03-cli.md) § Guard and warden
- **Created:** 2026-09-25
- **Dependencies:** PB-229
- **Cost:** major

## Context

Finding discovered while working on PB-229. PB-229 folds mail that lands under an outstanding knock, but the first message of a fresh mailbox still knocks at once. A hand-off burst therefore still wakes the orchestrator about twice: the first knock covers the records, the recipient takes them, and the `result` that lands after the take knocks again.

Evidence, measured on the PB-229 branch with the real `supervisorRound` (`lib/warden.js` `wardenRound`, one round per second, sends at +0, +3, +5, +18 s, the recipient taking its mailbox N s after each knock; "tie" is whether the take or the send comes first inside the same second):

| take after a knock | before PB-229 | with PB-229 |
|---|---|---|
| 5 s | 3 | 2 (send first) / 3 (take first) |
| 6–15 s | 4 | 2 |
| 20 s | 4 | 1 |

A quiet period before the FIRST knock was not built; its effect is a model, not a measurement. The model is PB-229's rule plus one condition on the first knock of a fresh mailbox, and at a zero quiet period it reproduces the measured row above (2 / 3 knocks for a 5 s take). On the same timing with a 5 s take:

| quiet period | fixed (first knock at `since` + Q) | debounce (first knock once no mail arrived for Q) | cost: first-knock delay of a lone message |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 s | 2–3 | 2–3 | 0 s |
| 3 s | 2 | 2 | 3 s |
| 5 s | 2 | 2 | 5 s |
| 10 s | 2 | 1 (knock at +15) | 10 s |
| 15 s | 1 (knock at +15) | 1 (knock at +33) | 15 s |

One knock needs the last send (+18) to land before the take that answers the first knock, so the first knock plus the take delay must pass +18: fixed 15 s knocks at +15 and the take comes at +20; debounce 10 s knocks 10 s after the +5 send, also at +15. Both land the `result` under the outstanding knock. Every message that arrives alone — an `answer`, a `question`, a `review` — pays the delay in full, and those are the messages whose turnaround the orchestrator waits on. An assumption, not checked: the gaps inside a hand-off depend on the worker's own turn, so a quiet period tuned on one burst may miss the next.

## Work to do

- Decide whether one fewer wake per hand-off is worth a delay on every lone message; if it is, choose the fixed or debounce form and its value, as a named constant beside `KNOCK_COALESCE_SEC`, with the reason in 03-cli § Guard and warden.

## Out of scope

- The coalescing of PB-229 itself: mail under an outstanding knock.
- The hand-off form (records first, then `result`).

## Verification

- Replay the +0/+3/+5/+18 timing against a 5 s take on the real round: the chosen quiet period gives the knock count the model predicts, and a lone message knocks no later than the quiet period.

## Deferred

- **Deferred:** 2026-09-25
- **Reason:** the numbers above come from a replay, not from a run. PB-229 already folds mail under an outstanding knock; whether the second knock of a hand-off still costs enough to delay every lone message is a trade the owner makes on measured runs. The 2026-09-26 consumer run below used a warden started before the release carrying PB-229 was installed, so it does not meet the return condition.
- **Return condition:** a run on a release carrying PB-229 whose `supervisor.log` shows hand-offs still knocking the orchestrator two or more times — the lines `notification <address>: unread N, knock K` counted per hand-off — quoted in this card with the consumer and its tracker named in words, the date and the count.

## Evidence — 2026-09-27

A consumer developer workspace's Promptobus task journal records a run started on 2026-09-26 with a live `supervisor.log`. The command below reads its lines through 2026-09-27 00:48 UTC (`SUPERVISOR_LOG` is that run's log); later appends do not change the count. It found 475 `notification orchestrator` lines: knock 1 ×388, 2 ×52, 3 ×16, 4 ×10, 5 ×4, 6 ×2, and 7, 8 and 9 ×1 each.

```sh
node -e 'const fs=require("fs"); const s=fs.readFileSync(process.argv[1],"utf8").split("\n").filter(x=>x.slice(0,24)<="2026-09-27T00:48:00.000Z"); const a=s.map(x=>x.match(/notification orchestrator: unread \d+, knock (\d+)/)).filter(Boolean).map(x=>+x[1]); console.log(a.length, Object.fromEntries([...new Set(a)].sort((x,y)=>x-y).map(k=>[k,a.filter(x=>x===k).length])))' "$SUPERVISOR_LOG"
```

One hand-off from a worker sent two artifacts at 18:29:39.685 and 18:29:43.742 UTC, then a result at 18:30:19.724 (the run's `messages/*.json` envelopes). `rg -n '^2026-09-26T18:(29|30):' "$SUPERVISOR_LOG"` shows three orchestrator notifications at 18:29:39.750, 18:29:43.751 and 18:30:19.747, with mailbox takes at 18:29:42.725, 18:29:45.563 and 18:30:22.553. Thus this hand-off used three knocks, each on a fresh mailbox after the prior take.

This is not yet the release measurement the return condition asks for. The warden started at 17:56:53 UTC from a consumer developer workspace installation whose dependency lock pinned `promptobus` v0.17.0. The installed v0.18.0 directory was created later, at 20:35 UTC. At v0.17.0 `src/supervisor.ts:540` knocks whenever unread grows; the current `:615` suppresses growth under an outstanding knock. A Node process keeps modules it loaded at start, so the older process's knocks cannot verify the new rule.

The apparent coalescing failure at 17:57:34.091 (knock 1) and 17:57:43.983 (knock 2) has no `delivered` line between them; the take is logged at 17:57:46.355. The second message arrived at 17:57:43.969, and the v0.17.0 `grew` branch explains the immediate second knock. There is no evidence of an unlogged take or a defect in PB-229's coalescing. Keep this card deferred until a restarted warden running v0.18.0 or later yields a hand-off count.
