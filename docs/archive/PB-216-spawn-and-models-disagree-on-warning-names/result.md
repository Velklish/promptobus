# PB-216 · Result

**Closed 2026-09-16, completed — and the card's own hypothesis was refuted by measurement.** The card read one live run in which `spawn` printed `warnings: unknown-remaining, near-limit, near-limit` while `models`, read a minute later, printed a detailed section without `near-limit`, and concluded that the two outputs keep two dictionaries — that the short line abbreviates what the detailed section spells out. They do not. There is one dictionary and it was already locked in three places: `DECISION_WARNINGS` in `lib/model-routing/resolver.js`, the `warningCode` enum in `schemas/model-routing/decision.schema.json`, and the code table in `docs/reference/03-cli.md`, with `test/model-routing.test.mjs` cross-checking the doc against the schema in both directions. Both outputs read the same `decision.warnings`: the short line through `routingMetadata` and `routingLine` in `lib/models.js`, the detailed section through `render`. Measured on four decisions, the two name sets agree character for character, order and multiplicity included.

**What actually differed were two decisions, not two dictionaries, and neither was said out loud.** `near-limit` is deliberately silent when the strategy it would recommend is the one already running — a warning that recommends what is already happening is noise — so one snapshot names it under `balance` and not under `economy`. And the second reading came sixty-five seconds later from a cache entry already past its TTL (`reason: 'stale_cache'`), which raises `snapshot-stale` and moves the harness to `unknown`: hence three `unknown-remaining` where `spawn` had one. Reading the two outputs as two renderings of one snapshot was the error, and it cost a live run on 2026-09-13.

**So the output now says both things.** `near-limit` states that it is a **pace** measurement and not a short form of `unknown-remaining` — that one says a harness exposes no limit source at all — and that it is not raised under the strategy it would propose. The explainer is printed **once**, under the `warnings:` heading in `render` (`NEAR_LIMIT_NOTE`), rather than repeated inside every `near-limit` line: the signal is per harness, the explanation is not. The same distinction went into the code table in `03-cli.md` and into the `README.md` line about `near-limit`.

**`lib/model-routing/resolver.js` is byte-for-byte identical to the base commit.** The first round put a clause at the end of the warning message there; review asked for the explainer to live in the renderer instead, and taking that whole — rather than in part — took the file out of a neighbouring track's zone altogether and returned the golden `decision.json` to its base content with it. What remains in that zone is `lib/model-routing/render.js`, seven lines, the reviewer's own variant. The golden `models.txt` gained one line and was regenerated by script per `test/fixtures/model-routing/README.md` § the goldens are regenerated, not hand-edited; every other byte reproduced unchanged.

**A hypothesis was chased and dropped without a card.** A `near-limit` was suspected to fire falsely on a window with a known `usedPercent` but no `resetAt`, where the level test would formally trip and the message would print `0.0 % elapsed` out of a `null`. Reproduced by hand on `balance-snapshot.json` with `usedPercent: 91.2` and `resetAt` removed: no warning appears, because `representatives` is built only from rows whose `pace` is eligible and a non-paceable row never reaches it. No finding was filed.
