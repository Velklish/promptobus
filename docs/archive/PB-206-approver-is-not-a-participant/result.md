# PB-206 · Result

**Closed 2026-09-13.** Done. Acceptance is a fourth addressed role, `approver:<slug>`, and it is addressable end to end: the protocol grammar, the store, the MCP surface, the lift map, the driver plans, the model-routing contracts and their schemas all know it, and a message from that address reaches the orchestrator and a worker. The decision is recorded as ADR-013.

**The fork was closed by the owner before the work started** — the role, not a verb — so this card implemented a decision rather than argued one. Why it does not repeat the option [ADR-012](../../adr/adr-012-stopping-one-participant-is-a-verb-of-its-own.md) rejected is stated in the new ADR: that decision refused a flag on `done` in favour of a verb, and the cleanup verb of PB-208 follows it; a role and a verb answer different questions, and the one refused there is not the one taken here.

**The map in the card was verified, not trusted, and it was short by six places.** Three further executable copies of the role list live in the `stallRoute` relift branches of the three drivers — without them a dead `approver:*` would have been offered a `spawn` and lifted back as a **worker**, silently changing the role on recovery. Three more were documentary. A seventh surface, `src/mcp/tools.ts`, was found the same way.

**Two defects that predate this work were found by the parity check written for it**, and that is what pays for the unification this card deliberately did not do: `overlay.schema.json` rejected `deny.byRole.approver` while the runtime accepted it, and `DEFAULT_POLICY.byRole` carried only two roles, so merging the third fell through before schema validation. Both would have shipped silently. The check now covers `RULE_ROLES`, `ROLES`, the floors, the catalog roles and role ratings, the overlay quality floor and `byRole`, the decision record and telemetry; three surface probes show it turns red naming the exact surface when a role is removed from any of them, and green again when restored. Unification of the copies is filed as [PB-206.4](../../backlog/queue/PB-206.4-model-routing-role-contract-has-independent-copies.md) rather than done here.

**The decisions the card left to the author, each with its reason in the ADR:** the quality floor of the role is 7, argued from what an approver does — work mechanical by recipe and irreversible in consequence — rather than by sitting between the other two numbers; the package deny list for the role is empty, and the reviewer deny lists are byte-for-byte unchanged under [ADR-009](../../adr/adr-009-reviewer-resolves-no-discrepancy.md); `routingPolicy` opens for worker↔approver traffic, and the ADR names that as a deliberate exception to the rule that participants do not talk to each other, together with what the orchestrator stops seeing in its own mailbox and where that traffic remains visible.

**An isolated review of the diff returned four findings, three major, and all four were resolved before the merge.** Two of them were the price of that opened route and of the new contract member, and neither was visible to the author or to the approver:

- the direct route was not limited to participants of the same task — an explicitly named foreign task plus auto-registration of an unknown sender let an address of one task write into another and be added there. Direct traffic now has to prove the sender is a participant held by the calling session; foreign auto-registration survives only for mail to `orchestrator`;
- a host-classified MCP deny would have made a Codex approver read-only, because the driver derived read-only from a non-empty deny list. It now derives it from the role and the permission mode, so denying one external tool no longer removes the repository writes without which there is no merged-tree gate, no squash and no archive. The same trap is closed for any future role;
- catalog validation checked the raw quality while routing applies the per-role override before the floor, so a row could pass `validate` and sit below the runtime floor. Effective quality is checked now, for the tuple and for the assessed base row;
- the fourth — the new address missing from the successor guard's participant predicate — was already closed in the main line by a neighbouring track, and the consequence the reviewer drew from it was measured on the merged tree with a negative control: a role the predicate does not know receives the takeover hint, `approver:a` receives `null`.

**Checks.** `npm test` on the exact handed-over commit `bf68f73`: exit 1, 57 of 64 files green, and the red set is exactly the seven files this environment cannot run — proved before the work began by fourteen runs, two per file, with the load average beside each. The set is identical before and after; no eighth file appeared, and the per-file deltas are accounted for (`promptobus-mcp` grew from 90/106 to 93/109 by three new green route checks). `build`, `audit`, `pins` and `backslop lint` exit 0. Eight mutation probes, each red with the mutation and green restored.

The seven red files are the subject of [PB-206.2](../PB-206.2-codex-participant-carries-two-harness-identities/task.md) and [PB-206.3](../../backlog/queue/PB-206.3-participant-sandbox-refuses-unix-socket-in-tmp.md) and belong to the sandbox, not to this change: the same suite is green in a participant sandbox of another harness, and removing one ambient environment variable turns two of the four identity files fully green.

**Left open.** The full suite on the merged tree and the acceptance itself are the approver's, not the author's. The consumer half — the run guide, the orchestration skill, the three-role ADR of the consumer and its spawn reference — is that repository's own card. A live run in which an addressed approver performs the acceptance, and the orchestrator's own calls for that run contain no gate run and no merge, is not made: it needs the consumer half first, and the card's own check says so.
